## When a claim is late

A claim submitted after the **30-day window** in the
[expense policy](expense-policy.md) is not caught by the policy's written
exception path ("in writing to the Finance Department").
[^owner-interview-2026-09-17]

In practice, the submission **form rejects the claim automatically** once the
30 days have passed.

## Getting HR to reconsider

An employee whose claim the form rejected emails HR with the **receipt
attached**. The email reaches HR directly.

## What HR does

HR looks at how many days late the claim is and decides whether to
**approve or reject** it. There is no fixed number written down for the
decision — HR weighs each claim on its days.

When HR approves, the payment comes **with the employee's salary** in the next
payroll, rather than through the standard expense-reimbursement path in the
expense policy.

> [!NOTE]
> This procedure differs from the written exception in the expense policy,
> which says an exception is granted in writing by the Finance Department.
> Here the approver is HR, reached by email. The record stores both as stated;
> which one governs is a question for the owner.

## Open questions

- Whether there is any ceiling on how late a claim may be for HR to approve
  it — the owner said "30" while describing the window, but no number was
  confirmed as a second cap.
- Whether HR attaches a warning when approving a late claim ("maybe warning").
- Who in HR — a role, a person, or any mailbox reader — makes the decision.
- Whether the written policy's Finance Department path is meant to replace
  this, or this replaces it.
